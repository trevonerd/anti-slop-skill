# Writing like a person (and keeping SEO)

Wikipedia warns against only "removing the signs": that hides the problem without fixing it. The real problem is empty or wrong content [W1]. So this guide is about better writing, never about fooling a detector.

## Ten rules

1. Start from a fact only you know. The track name, the real instrument, the real duration, the moment the song changes, where the idea came from. A specific, checkable detail is the opposite of regression to the mean [W1][MAC].
2. Say it plainly. "is" and "has" instead of "serves as" and "boasts" [W1]. Orwell: the short word over the long one, cut what can be cut, no metaphor you have seen in print a thousand times [ORW]. People write "there is", "it has", plain verbs (wrote, used, tried), flat superlatives ("the only", "the first") and qualifiers such as "very" or "perhaps" more often [W1]. <!-- slop-scan: ignore-line -->
3. One voice, one opinion. First person when it fits, a clear preference ("the third track is my favourite for the first coffee"), a small anecdote. Graphite finds these traits in human text: first person, "my favorite", "last year", casual qualifiers such as "pretty", exclamation marks, asides in brackets [GRA].
4. Vary the rhythm. A long sentence, then a short one. Not three fragments in a row, not every sentence at twelve words.
5. Cut filler. Prefaces ("it's worth noting"), recap closers, trailing "-ing" clauses ("..., creating a cozy vibe"), corrective contrasts, lists of three that repeat one idea [UNS][HUM][ECO]. <!-- slop-scan: ignore-line -->
6. Repeat when the right word repeats. If the word is "jazz", use it twice. Forced variation sounds false [UNS][W1].
7. Show, do not praise. Instruments, time, place instead of "perfect", "unforgettable", "magical" [W1].
8. Verify every fact, metadata included [GGL].
9. Make each piece in a series different for real: a different moment, detail, scene or opinion, not the same mould with a new noun [YT1].
10. Read it aloud. If the author would not say it like that to a friend, rewrite it [SCR].

## Voice

- If there is a writing sample, read it first and match its sentence length, word choice, punctuation, openings and slang. The sample wins over the general rules, including the dash rule.
- Deliberate slang, dialect and catchphrases the author uses are voice. Keep them at the author's rate; do not add more than they would.
- Without a sample, take the voice from the genre: personal posts keep opinions, doubts and humour; reference and technical text stays neutral and plain.
- Do not invent personality. An opinion is allowed when the voice calls for one; a fact is not.

## Rhythm and punctuation

- Normal, varied punctuation: commas, a bracket now and then, an exclamation mark when it is sincere, a dash when it really helps. Removing every dash has become a pattern too [W1][ECO].
- Paragraphs of different lengths. Stop when you have finished; no summary paragraph.

## Language notes

- Italian: Think in Italian, do not translate [SCR]. Use clitics and spoken forms ("ci penso io", "non ne posso più"), fillers in moderation ("allora", "insomma"), dislocations ("il caffè lo prendo dopo"), irony [HUM]. No English-style spaced dash, no capitalised months, no comma before "e" in lists [TRC][HUM].
- Spanish: Pick one regional variety and keep it (tuteo or voseo, "vosotros" or "ustedes") [ESS]. Opening "¿" and "¡".
- French: French typography: « » quotes, a space before ":" ";" "!" "?" [FRD].
- German: Fewer chained connectors; a clear position instead of perfect balance [DEB].

## Read-aloud test

Three questions, adapted from a German editorial guide [DEB]:
1. Does it name something concrete that only a person with experience would know?
2. Does it dare a clear opinion somewhere (where the genre allows one)?
3. Does it have one irregularity: a break, an aside, an uneven sentence?

Three "no" answers mean the text needs rewriting, whoever wrote it.
